Spaced retrieval practice in doctoral reading groups
Okafor & Lindqvist · 2023 · J. Higher Education Research 41(2) · p. 118 of 19

We recruited 142 first- and second-year doctoral students across nine universities and assigned reading groups to either massed or spaced review of the same twelve articles.

Recall at six weeks was highest when review sessions were spaced three to seven days apart, and the effect was larger for students reading outside their home discipline.

Participants were followed for twelve weeks only, so we cannot say whether the advantage persists through a full thesis cycle. Does spacing help synthesis as well as recall?

How it works

From a folder of PDFs to a chapter you can defend.

  1. Upload your papers

    Drop in PDFs. ResearchOS reads each one and gets it ready to search, so there is nothing to set up.

  2. Read and mark

    Open papers in the reader, highlight by meaning and write notes in the margin as you go.

  3. Ask and compare

    Ask questions across your library, then look at the gap table to see what is still unstudied.

  4. Write with citations

    Copy references in the right style, with the notes and passages behind each claim close at hand.

Who it is for

Made for the first three years of a PhD, when the reading never stops.

If you are working through a literature review and losing weeks to scattered tools, ResearchOS is meant for you. Every feature has to make that stretch of research easier, or it does not belong. Shared libraries and a mobile app are planned for later.
